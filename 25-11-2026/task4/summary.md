# Employee Attrition Analysis – Summary Report

## 1. Problem
The objective of this project is to analyse employee data, understand
employee attrition patterns, compare departments and identify factors
that may help improve employee retention.

## 2. Data
- Dataset: IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition
- Total employees analysed: 1,470
- Total columns: 35
- Missing values after cleaning: 0
- Duplicate records after cleaning: 0

## 3. Key Insights

1. Overall Attrition:
   The overall employee attrition rate is 16.12%.

2. Overtime and Attrition:
   Employees working overtime have an attrition rate of 30.53%,
   compared with 10.44% for employees not working overtime.

3. Department Salary Comparison:
   Sales has the highest average monthly income at 6,959.17,
   followed by Human Resources at 6,654.51 and Research &
   Development at 6,281.25.

## 4. Recommendations

1. Employee Retention:
   Review workload, overtime practices and employee satisfaction
   to reduce attrition among employees working overtime.

2. Department Analysis:
   Regularly monitor salary distribution and employee attrition
   across departments to identify areas requiring attention.

## 5. Limitations

- The analysis is based on a single employee dataset containing
  1,470 records.
- The dataset does not establish that correlations cause attrition.
- The analysis reflects the available dataset and may not represent
  all organisations.
- Department budget figures used in the merge analysis are
  illustrative, not actual company financial data.

## Conclusion

The analysis indicates that overtime is associated with a higher
employee attrition rate. Organisations should monitor workload,
employee retention and department-level trends to support
better workforce decisions.
